How to hire a remote assistant from Latin America

Outsource Your Tasks · November 25, 2026

Start from the hours the seat has to cover and the language mix it needs, and write the tasks and the tools down before you read a resume. Test written English and spoken English on a live call, plus Spanish if the role needs it, with an exercise taken from the job. Check the setup, the connection, and a reference, and plan the first weeks of management before you hire.

This is for a US owner of a law firm, a home-services company, or another small business, hiring directly, through a marketplace, or with a placement firm.

What should you decide before you start looking?

Write the tasks, the hours, and the languages before you read a resume. Use that page as the post, the brief, and the scorecard.

Which tasks and tools will they own?

List the week they will finish, and the work that stays with you. A paralegal keeps the file, drafts you review, deadlines, records, and approved updates, under attorney supervision. An intake coordinator takes the new call and books the consult. A home-services seat answers and books on the board you already use, such as ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, or Jobber. Elsewhere, name the inbox, the calendar, the follow-up, and the logins.

Which hours do you need covered?

Name the days and the clock in your time zone, for example Monday to Friday, 8:30 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. Eastern, live when you open. Say if you need your own hours, an early start, or evenings only, and who they answer.

Do you need English only, or English and Spanish?

English is for working with you. Add Spanish when callers or crews need it, and say which language is spoken and which is in the file. Many offices speak Spanish and write the record in English.

Which Latin American time zones line up with US hours?

These cities keep one clock all year. Most of the United States changes clocks in March and November, so the gap moves by one hour. Put your hours in your time zone in the offer, and the same shift in their local time.

Mexico City is UTC-6 all year. Mexico ended daylight saving time in most of the country in 2022. A few northern-border municipalities still follow the US clock, so name the city. Colombia (Bogotá) and Peru (Lima) are UTC-5 with no daylight saving time. Argentina (Buenos Aires) and Brazil (São Paulo) are UTC-3 with no daylight saving time.

Eastern Time is UTC-5 in winter and UTC-4 in summer. Central, Mountain, and Pacific are one, two, and three hours behind Eastern. Arizona and Hawaii do not spring forward.

How do you check English level?

Test speaking and writing separately, on a live call, with part of it unscripted. Leave a silence, ask an unscripted follow-up, then role-play a real case: a missing record, no heat, the next job, or a missed callback. You want accurate facts and your decisions left with you.

The same day, ask for the follow-up email from four or five bullet notes. Send it only if a light edit is enough and it promises nothing you did not say.

On the Common European Framework (CEFR), B2 is a routine conversation and a clear email. A fast or upset caller can still be hard to follow, so B2 fits internal work you review. C1 is a professional call that can change subject, writing you would lightly edit, and a clear retelling. Listen for C1 when they own the phone. C2 is near-native nuance, for writing in your voice most of the day.

How do you check Spanish if your callers or crews speak it?

Hear one live case in Spanish, an approved update, a leak, or a late crew, then the same story in English. Listen for facts and for a question instead of an invented promise. If you do not speak Spanish, include someone who does. Say what they may tell the caller. You still decide.

What should vetting include beyond the interview?

Before the offer, check the work and the setup.

  • A work sample in your workflow, and whether they ask or guess.
  • A walk-through of your tools. New software can be week-one learning if you expect it.
  • Internet during your hours, a backup for power or connectivity, and a quiet room.
  • A reference, from a US employer when they have one, on hours, English, and a repaired mistake.
  • The schedule in writing, with the time zone and how they report a day off.

What does it take to manage a remote assistant well?

You set the priorities. Week one is accounts, permissions, and a short written procedure for each task. Then a daily check-in, a shared task list, and a 30-day review against your original list. Attorneys still supervise legal work. You still decide what dispatch may book.

Should you hire directly, use a marketplace, or work with a placement firm?

Direct hiring shows every applicant, and you screen them. A marketplace starts from profiles. Treat the profile as a claim, and ask who else has their hours if you want someone dedicated to you. A placement firm brings a shorter list from your brief. Interview them yourself. After the hire, you decide and you manage.

What roles do small businesses usually fill this way?

Law firms usually fill a virtual paralegal seat or a legal intake coordinator seat. The page for small law firms sets them side by side. Home-services companies usually fill a virtual dispatcher seat, sometimes beside a scheduling coordinator. Other businesses fill inbox, calendar, and follow-up. One person, your hours, your tools.

Questions owners ask

Is Latin America a good place to hire a remote assistant for a US business?

Yes, when you need someone on a US business day and you hire for those hours on purpose. Mexico City, Bogotá, Lima, Buenos Aires, and São Paulo keep fixed clocks that overlap a US office. Tasks, languages, and your management of the person decide the fit.

What time zone overlap can I expect with a Latin American assistant?

Bogotá and Lima match US Eastern in winter and are one hour behind in summer. Mexico City is one hour behind Eastern in winter and two hours behind in summer. Buenos Aires and São Paulo are two hours ahead of Eastern in winter and one hour ahead in summer. US clocks change in March and November. These cities do not. Write the hours in your time zone and name the city.

What English level should a remote assistant have?

Strong enough to follow on an unscripted call, and strong enough that light edits are enough for the writing. Test speech and writing separately. Phone seats need a professional conversation. Reviewed internal work can sit at a solid intermediate level.

How do I vet a virtual assistant before hiring?

Use a work sample from your workflow, walk through the tools, and confirm internet, a backup connection, and a quiet place for calls. Speak with a reference, a US employer when they have one, and put the schedule in writing.

Can a Latin American assistant answer calls in Spanish and English?

Yes, when you hire for both and hear both. They can take the Spanish call and report it in English. You still decide what they may say.

Do I need to manage a remote assistant differently from an in-office employee?

You set priorities either way. Write down accounts and permissions, a short procedure per task, a daily check-in, a shared task list, and a 30-day review.

What tools does a remote assistant need to work with my team?

The tools you already use: email, phone, calendar, files, and your board or practice software. They need their own login, a working computer, and a connection that holds during your hours.

What do you bring to the first conversation?

Bring the tasks, the hours in your time zone, and the languages. You keep the decisions. Book a discovery call when you want the search handled. The call is complimentary, and the subject is the role.
