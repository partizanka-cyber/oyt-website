Latin America vs. the Philippines: Where Should a US Small Business Hire a Remote Assistant?

Outsource Your Tasks · November 27, 2026

Latin America usually fits best when you need same-business-day overlap with US hours and Spanish-English bilingual work, such as client calls and intake. The Philippines is a strong option for overnight and asynchronous back-office work, and for an English-only team on a handoff. You still hire the person whose hours, languages, and tasks match the seat.

Owners usually call this person a virtual assistant. This page is for the owner of a US small business or nonprofit, with law firms first and home-services companies close behind.

Which time zones overlap with US business hours?

Most of the United States changes clocks in March and November. The cities below do not, so the gap moves by one hour twice a year. Write the shift in your time zone and in local time, and name the city.

Mexico City is UTC-6 all year. Mexico ended daylight saving time in most of the country in 2022, though a few northern-border municipalities still follow the US clock. Colombia (Bogotá) and Peru (Lima) are UTC-5. Argentina (Buenos Aires) and Brazil (São Paulo) are UTC-3. Manila is UTC+8. US Eastern Time is UTC-5 in winter and UTC-4 in summer. Central, Mountain, and Pacific are one, two, and three hours behind Eastern. Arizona and Hawaii do not spring forward.

A 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. Eastern day is a normal day in Mexico City, Bogotá, Lima, Buenos Aires, and São Paulo. The same day is a night shift in Manila: 10 p.m. to 6 a.m. in winter, and 9 p.m. to 5 a.m. in summer. A normal Manila daytime runs through the US night. A full live day for a Pacific office is a night shift in the Philippines, so agree to it before you interview.

Which is better for bilingual Spanish-English work?

Choose Latin America when a caller, a client, or a crew needs Spanish. Spanish is the everyday language in Mexico, Colombia, Peru, and Argentina. Brazil's everyday language is Portuguese, so name a Spanish-speaking country when Spanish is part of the seat. The person takes the call in Spanish and reports it in English. Hear both on one live case. If you do not speak Spanish, include someone who does. You still decide what they may say.

Filipino and English are the official languages of the Philippines, and Spanish is not the working language there. A Spanish-speaking caller is a reason to hire in Latin America, including a legal intake coordinator. An English-only seat can come from either region. Test the person, and let the hours choose the country.

How does English proficiency compare?

A country is not an English level. In the Philippines, English is an official language and a daily working language in school, government, and a large professional-services workforce, so an English-only seat is often easier to fill. In Latin America, English is a learned skill and the level varies by person. A phone seat needs a conversation that can change subject. Writing you review should need only a light edit.

Test both regions the same way. Leave a silence, ask an unscripted follow-up, and role-play a missing record or a missed callback. The same day, ask for the follow-up email from a few notes you provide.

Which is better for law firms and client-facing roles?

Client-facing means someone is reachable while your office is open: intake, and the client update you approved. A paralegal works under attorney supervision, and you stay the lawyer. Your jurisdiction sets the rules for the work.

That live seat usually fits Latin America. The business day overlaps, so a Spanish-speaking client can be called while you are in a hearing. The Philippines can cover English-only calls when the person has agreed to your hours. Put both clocks in the offer before the interview. A firm can also split the work: live Spanish-English calls in Latin America, and records and drafts ready before you open on a Philippine day.

Which is better for back-office or overnight tasks?

Back-office work moves without a live conversation: the inbox, a draft from your notes, records you asked for, and tomorrow's board. You leave a clear list, and the work is further along when you return. The Philippines fits that on a normal local day, because daytime in Manila is the US night. Latin America fits the same tasks when a question has to be answered before you close.

A virtual dispatcher who answers while the trucks are out needs your open hours, which points to Latin America. Someone who confirms tomorrow's route and clears overnight messages can sit in the Philippines. You still decide what they may promise. If both lists are long, hire two seats.

How do I decide?

Write three lines before you pick a region. The hours, in your time zone, and whether someone must be live for all of them. The languages, and which one is on the call. Whether the work is a live conversation, a handoff, or both.

Questions owners ask

Are Filipino or Latin American virtual assistants better?

Neither region is better on its own. Latin America usually fits a shared business day and Spanish-English work such as calls and intake. The Philippines is a strong option for overnight, English-only work you can hand off. Hire for the hours, the languages, and the tasks, then test the person.

What time zone do Latin American virtual assistants work in?

Mexico City stays on UTC-6, Bogotá and Lima on UTC-5, and Buenos Aires and São Paulo on UTC-3. These cities do not change clocks. A US Eastern business day overlaps a normal local day in each of them. Name the city in the offer.

Can a virtual assistant from the Philippines work US hours?

Yes, when you hire for those hours and the person agrees. The Philippines stays on UTC+8, so a US Eastern day is a night shift: 13 hours ahead in winter and 12 hours ahead in summer. A normal daytime shift there covers US evening and overnight.

Is it legal to hire a remote assistant from another country?

Many US businesses hire people who live in another country. Employee, payroll provider, or contractor depends on their country, your state, and the work. This page is not legal advice. Ask a lawyer or a provider who handles cross-border hiring before you make an offer.

How do I vet a remote assistant?

Use a work sample from your workflow, a live call with an unscripted follow-up, and a writing check the same day. Confirm a quiet place, internet during your hours, and a backup. Speak with a reference, and write down the schedule and the languages.

Which countries in Latin America are best for remote assistants?

There is no single best country. Mexico, Colombia, and Peru line up closely with US Eastern and Central hours. Argentina and Brazil run ahead of US Eastern and still cover a US business day, including later into a Pacific evening. Pick the city whose clock matches the shift, then hire the person.

Is the Philippines better for English-only work?

It is often easier to look there when the seat is English only and the work can happen overnight. English is an official language and a daily working language for many professionals. You still test the person. A live seat during your business day is a simpler schedule in Latin America.

Should a law firm hire in Latin America or the Philippines?

Start in Latin America when someone must be on the phone during office hours, especially in Spanish and English. Use the Philippines when files and drafts should be ready before the office opens. A paralegal works under attorney supervision either way.
