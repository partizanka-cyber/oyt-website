A virtual paralegal supports matters that are already open, from a desk that is not in your office. They organize the file, prepare drafts for your review, track deadlines, request records, and update clients with the status you have approved. A paralegal works under attorney supervision at your firm. You stay the lawyer.

This page is for the owner who is still doing that work after hours, or who needs someone who can speak with Spanish-speaking clients while the office is open. If the real problem is the new call, look at a legal intake coordinator first. Both seats sit on the page for small law firms.

What they take off your desk

Draw the line with the attorney who will supervise. Once that line is clear, owners commonly hand a virtual paralegal work like this.

  • Building and maintaining the matter file.
  • Drafting routine letters and forms for your review before anything goes out.
  • Requesting records and tracking what has come back.
  • Keeping the deadline calendar, and flagging a date while there is still time to act.
  • Scheduling clients, experts, and depositions.
  • Summarizing documents so you can read the summary before the pile.
  • Calling clients with the update you approved, in English or in Spanish.
  • Getting a hearing file ready with time to spare.

You decide which tasks stay on the attorney's desk. A strong paralegal pauses and asks when a task needs a lawyer's judgment. Treat that question as a sign of fit. The hiring guide turns this list into the brief you can take into a search, including what to listen for in both languages.

Spanish on the call, English in the file

If a client wants the update in Spanish, and the only person who can give it is in a hearing, that client waits. A bilingual paralegal can hold the conversation in Spanish and tell you, in English, what was said. Many small firms keep the file in English and use Spanish on the call. Say which is which before the search starts, so the person you meet already works that way.

You should hear both languages yourself. A short role-play tells you more than a line on a resume: a client asks whether the records arrived, the honest answer is that one provider has not sent them, and then the candidate explains the call to you in English. Listen for facts, for a legal answer left with you, and for English you can supervise.

Why Latin America, for this seat

OYT looks to college-educated professionals in Latin America when a firm needs the working day to overlap a US office. Much of the region shares that business day, so a remote hire can be at their desk while you are at yours. The time zone is agreed up front. It is not a surprise on the first Monday.

The person works only for your firm. You set the hours, the matters, and the review path. They learn the calendar and the file system you already use. Court runs and clients who are in your conference room still need someone local. The file, the drafts, and the client update can live on a remote desk.

Questions owners ask

What the role covers, who supervises it, and how a bilingual search is run.

What is a virtual paralegal?

A virtual paralegal supports open matters from a remote desk: the file, drafts for your review, deadlines, records, and client updates you have approved. A paralegal works under attorney supervision at your firm. You keep the legal judgment.

Can a remote paralegal work with Spanish-speaking clients?

Yes, when you hire for a bilingual seat. They can give a client the update you approved, in Spanish, and tell you in English what was said. You still decide what they may say. Spanish is the language of the call. It is not a substitute for your review.

Who supervises the legal work?

Your attorneys do. Outsource Your Tasks sources, screens, and introduces the person. After you hire, you set the work and you manage the day. OYT does not direct legal work.

Will their hours overlap my office?

For this role, OYT looks to Latin America because the region shares the business day with US firms. The time zone is agreed before anyone starts, so you are choosing someone who can be available while your office is open.

How does OYT vet candidates?

Each search is run fresh for your role. OYT checks that the experience fits, evaluates English, and confirms the computer, internet, and backup are ready for remote work. Finalists are interviewed by OYT. You receive a resume, a DISC profile, and a recorded video interview before you meet them.

How is this different from an intake coordinator?

A virtual paralegal supports matters that are already open. A legal intake coordinator handles the new lead: the first call, your screening questions, the entry in your system, and the booked consult. Many firms later add the second seat. Both are on the page for small law firms.
