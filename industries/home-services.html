What does a virtual dispatcher do for an HVAC company?

A virtual dispatcher is on the phone and the board while your techs are in a mechanical room, an attic, or a truck between calls. They are not a second technician, and they do not diagnose a system.

What happens on the live call?

They take the address, the equipment, and what the customer is seeing. They ask the questions you wrote, check who is closest and who has the right skill, and book the arrival window your rules allow. The notes go on the job so the tech is not walking in blind.

What do they update on the dispatch board?

They confirm tomorrow's jobs, move a window when a repair runs long, and tell the next customer before that person is waiting in the driveway. If a part will not arrive, they reschedule instead of leaving a dead job on the board. The point of the seat is fewer missed jobs: a call gets a time, and a change of plan is a call from your company. What they may promise stays in a short script you can change.

What does a virtual scheduling coordinator do for roofing and electrical companies?

A dispatcher lives in the hour the phone rings. A virtual scheduling coordinator lives in the day and the week: runs built from jobs you have already sold, the confirmation, and the move when the plan breaks. One person can do both, or you can split the live call from tomorrow's board.

How does the day change for a roofing company?

Rain pushes a tear-off, a dumpster lands late, or a homeowner is not there for a drop. The coordinator confirms the afternoon before, writes down access, and slides the crew so two crews are not promised the same start. They do not get on the roof.

How does the day change for an electrical company?

A panel change may need the homeowner there, a utility window may be fixed, or two jobs may want the same van. The coordinator lines up who has to be home, how long you said the job takes, and which stop is first, then moves the later appointment when a job runs over. The same pattern fits an HVAC maintenance route.

Why hire a bilingual dispatcher for a plumbing company?

A leak does not wait for the caller to find the English word. Many customers would rather explain a backup, a burst line, or a water heater in Spanish, and they hang up when the only option is voicemail. A bilingual dispatcher for a plumbing company can take that call and book the job in the system you already use. Bilingual means English and Spanish, both strong enough for a real call. Hear both when you interview. You still decide what they may promise, which neighborhoods get a truck, and when they get you on the line.

How is a remote scheduling coordinator different from an answering service?

An answering service takes a message. Someone at your company still has to turn it into a job. A remote scheduling coordinator books the appointment, sends the confirmation, and reschedules on your board, so the technician sees the change. You hire them for your company alone. They learn your service area, your job types, and how you want a customer spoken to. An answering service can cover nights you do not staff. It does not run the board. A remote dispatcher for a home-services company does that work on the live call.
