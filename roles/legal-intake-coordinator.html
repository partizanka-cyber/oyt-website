The front door of a small firm is the new call. A bilingual legal intake coordinator stands there. They answer, they ask what you told them to ask, they enter the person in the system you already use, and they put the consult on your calendar. When the caller wants the conversation in Spanish, they can have it.

Firms sometimes look for outsourced legal intake and picture a shared desk that also answers for other companies. That is a different model. The person OYT places is dedicated to your firm. You manage the day. Your questions, your calendar, and your standard for a complete lead are the job.

This seat is not a paralegal. Intake is the new lead. A virtual paralegal supports matters that are already open, under attorney supervision. If the files are the problem, start there. What a legal intake coordinator handles

Write the questions before you write the job post. The coordinator's value is a complete, accurate lead, not a legal opinion. Owners commonly include work like this.

  • Answering new calls and new messages.
  • Speaking with Spanish-speaking callers and with English-speaking callers.
  • Asking the screening questions you approved.
  • Entering the lead in the system you already use.
  • Booking the consult on your calendar.
  • Sending the confirmation and the reminder you approved.
  • Writing down anything that needs a lawyer before anyone is told what a matter is worth.
  • Keeping a simple record of who called and whether they were booked.

They follow your questions. The decision to take a matter stays with the attorney. A question that is not on the list comes to you before the caller is given an answer. The hiring guide is written for the paralegal seat, and it is still useful here: hear both languages, and be specific about hours before you meet anyone.

Spanish-speaking leads

A caller who cannot get through in Spanish often calls the next firm. A bilingual coordinator can take that first conversation and write it up in English for you. You approve what they may say, the same way you would for an English call. Language changes who can talk. It does not change who decides whether the firm should take the matter.

Hear the Spanish yourself before you hire. Give them a short new-caller scenario and listen for a calm report that stays with the facts. Then ask them to tell you the same call in English. You are checking that you can supervise the work, not that a certificate says bilingual.

Hours that match the phone

Intake only helps if someone is there while your lines are open. OYT looks to college-educated professionals in Latin America for this role because the region shares the business day with US firms. The schedule is part of the search. Rate and time zone are presented before you decide, so you are not hiring someone who can only answer the phone after you have gone home.

They learn your calendar and your client system. You do not have to move the firm onto a new stack of tools. Introduce them to the people who used to catch these calls, and say which calls now move. Otherwise the old habit continues and the new person waits.

Questions owners ask

What intake covers, how Spanish-speaking leads are handled, and who manages the person after they start.

What does a legal intake coordinator do?

They handle the new lead. They answer the call or message, ask the screening questions you wrote, enter the person in the system you already use, and book the consult. They follow your questions. The decision to take a matter stays with the attorney.

How do they handle Spanish-speaking leads?

On a bilingual seat, they can take the first conversation in Spanish and record it for you in English. You still approve what they may promise. A caller who wants Spanish should not have to wait for the only bilingual person in a hearing.

Will they use our calendar and our system?

Yes. They learn the calendar, the files, and the client list you already use. You do not rebuild the office around a new set of tools. The strategy call is where you name the systems the role has to live in.

Who manages them?

You do. The person works only for your firm. OYT runs the search, the screening, and the introduction. After you hire, you set the priorities and you oversee the day.

How is this different from a virtual paralegal?

Intake is the front door. A virtual paralegal supports open matters under attorney supervision: the file, the drafts you review, and the deadline calendar. If the files are the problem, start with the paralegal. If new callers are the problem, start here.

Will their hours overlap our business day?

OYT looks to Latin America for this role so the working day can overlap a US office. The schedule is part of the search, and it is on the table before you decide. Phone coverage only helps if someone is there while your lines are open.

Tell us how the new calls should go

Bring the questions you wish every caller were asked, even if the list is still rough. The strategy call is complimentary.
